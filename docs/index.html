Why it sounds right

A caption boundary is not a speech boundary.

Speech-to-text wraps subtitle lines at a fixed width, so one sentence routinely spans two or three captions. This is where most tools go wrong.

30…brings together practical clinical references across
31pediatrics and neonatology. These include emergency resources…

✕  One clip per subtitle

The narrator stops mid-sentence at caption 30, then pads the rest of the window with silence. You get a robotic pause every few seconds, and long holes wherever the speech runs short.

✓  Narration Studio

Captions that continue the same sentence are grouped and spoken as one breath. Captions still change on their own timestamps — the voice simply does not stop when they do.

How it works

From a recorded video to a finished voice track.

Four things happen before you open the app, and the app hands you the prompt for the fiddly one.

1

Record your video with your own voice on it

Speak the script normally. The recording is only used to get an accurate transcript with timings — this app replaces the voice afterwards.

2

Ask ChatGPT for a subtitle file

Upload the video and ask for an .srt with timings. Any transcription tool works — Whisper, your editor's auto-captions — as long as you get real start and end times.

3

Clean up the subtitle text

Transcribers mis-hear names and punctuate badly, which is the biggest cause of narration that sounds wrong. The app ships a ready-made prompt that fixes the wording while keeping every timestamp identical.

4

Open Narration Studio

Choose a voice, generate, play the whole thing back, then export the audio for your editor. Six guided steps, each explaining what happens next.

What you get

Built around one promise: your timings never move.

🎯

Zero drift, by design

Every segment is written at its own start sample, never after the previous one's audio. If one renders short, nothing else shifts.

🗣️

A library of voices

Male and female, warm, bright, deep, calm. Preview any of them on a sample line before you commit.

🔒

Nothing leaves your machine

All speech is generated locally. No account, no upload, no telemetry, no analytics. Works offline after setup.

⏱️

Honest time fitting

Speech too long is compressed with pitch preserved, never truncated. Too short is stretched to fill rather than padded with dead air.

📝

Your words, your terms

A terminology dictionary you control, plus a separate pronunciation layer that changes what the voice says without touching the caption.

⚠️

It tells you what happened

Pre-flight checks before generating, plain-language errors with a recommended fix, and technical detail on request. Never a silent failure.

🎬

Editor-ready output

24 kHz 16-bit WAV or MP3 whose length matches your subtitle timeline exactly. Drop it straight into Kdenlive, Premiere or Resolve.

💾

Fast to iterate

Generated audio is cached by content, so editing one caption regenerates that section only — not the whole video.

What you need.

Python3.12 or newer — python.org. On Windows tick “Add python.exe to PATH”.
FFmpegbrew install ffmpeg on macOS · winget install Gyan.FFmpeg on Windows
Disk spaceAbout 3 GB for the speech engine, downloaded once on first launch
InputAn .srt subtitle file with real timings. Plain .txt and .md also work.

First launch takes a few minutes

The download is small because the speech engine is fetched once, on the first run, rather than bundled. macOS opens a Terminal window so you can watch it; Windows shows a console. Every launch after that is immediate.

Neither installer is code-signed, so macOS needs right-click ▸ Open the first time, and Windows SmartScreen needs More info ▸ Run anyway. Code signing requires paid developer certificates on both platforms.
